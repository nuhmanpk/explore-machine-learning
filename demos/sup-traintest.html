Challenges

Peek at the exam: press the button and watch both scores hit 100%. Is that real?
Noisier cards: set noise to 0.55 or more with 20+ study cards. The exam score stays much lower.
Unlucky split: deal 5 different splits with the same settings. Does the exam score stay the same?

What just happened?

  • The model only learned from the study cards, then was graded on exam cards it had never seen.
  • Looking things up from study cards gives 100% on the study cards. That score tells you very little.
  • The exam score is the honest one. With few study cards it is low; with more, it climbs and the gap shrinks.
  • Peeking at the exam cards makes both scores look great, but it is cheating. It no longer tests anything new.
  • A different random split gives a different exam score, so one number is a bit lucky or unlucky.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

The robot is a 1-nearest-neighbour classifier (k-NN with k = 1): it predicts the label of the closest training point. It has zero bias towards a smooth boundary, so it fits the training set perfectly (training accuracy 100% unless two identical points disagree). That is memorisation, and it is an overfit model by construction on noisy data.

  • The data are the two moons with Gaussian noise. The pool of 100 cards is shuffled and cut into a train and a test (hold-out) set; the slider sets the split.
  • Generalisation error is estimated on the test set. As training data grows, test accuracy rises toward the Bayes-rate limit set by the noise (the learning curve).
  • Data leakage: letting test cards into training inflates the test score to 100% and says nothing about new data. We also show a score on 300 fresh cards that never touched training.
  • The test score of one split is a noisy estimate (small test sets have high variance). Remedies: repeated splits, k-fold cross-validation, and keeping one final untouched test set.
