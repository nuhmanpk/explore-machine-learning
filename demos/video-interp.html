Challenges

Ghost hunter: choose Fade and put the time slider near the middle. Can you see two see-through squares? Try this: play the Fade at 8× slow-mo. Things don't move at all: one copy fades out while another fades in.
Super slow-mo: with speed 2× or more, choose Follow arrows and play it all the way through at 8× slow-mo. Try this: play Repeat at 8× too. It just sits still and then jumps: that's the jerky look of cheap slow motion.
Find the tear: choose Follow arrows with Perfect arrows, switch to the Oops map, and tap a spot that is still red. Try this: drag the time slider. The red bits sit just behind and in front of moving things, where the background was hidden in one of the pictures.

What just happened?

  • Repeat just shows picture A, then picture B. No new information, so the slow-mo looks jerky.
  • Fade mixes the two pictures like see-through paper. Moving things show up twice, faded: ghosts!
  • Follow arrows first works out where every bit moved (the motion arrows), then puts each bit part of the way along its arrow. Things really slide.
  • Even perfect arrows leave small mistakes where something was hidden in one picture, like the background just behind a moving ball. Nobody saw it there, so it has to be guessed.

How it works

Block matching finds which way each bit moved, just like Spot the Motion.

Pick a moment

Halfway? Then each bit should be halfway along its arrow.

Fetch from both

For every dot, look back along the arrow into A and forward into B, and fetch the colour from both.

Mix

Mix the two colours, using more of the nearer picture. Repeat for every made-up frame.

For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is video frame interpolation (frame-rate up-conversion), used for slow motion, 24→60 fps TV "motion smoothing" and video codecs (B-frames).

  • Frame repetition (sample-and-hold) is It = I0 for t ≤ ½, else I1: judder.
  • Cross-fade is It = (1−t)I0 + tI1. Its error at moving edges is a double exposure ("ghosting"). It minimises MSE only when there is no motion.
  • Motion-compensated interpolation uses backward warping: for each output pixel p it finds a vector v passing through p at time t, then samples (bilinearly) I0(p − t·v) and I1(p + (1−t)·v) and blends them with weights 1−t and t. Since the flow is estimated on frame A's grid, not at time t, we pick v per pixel from nearby flow vectors by minimising a symmetric (bilateral) 3×3 SAD between the two fetches, a classic TV-chip approach. Robo's arrows are 8×8 block matches (search ±12, sub-pixel), refined per pixel.
  • Occlusion: background uncovered between A and B exists in only one frame, so blending both fetches mixes foreground into it (the "tear" or halo). The fix is visibility-aware blending: estimate occlusion masks and fetch only from the frame where the pixel is visible. This demo deliberately leaves that out so you can see the artefact; the "Perfect arrows" mode shows it is not a flow-accuracy problem.
  • Deep interpolators: Super SloMo approximates intermediate flows from bidirectional flow and learns soft visibility maps; DAIN adds depth to decide which object is in front when vectors collide; RIFE directly predicts intermediate flows with a fast coarse-to-fine network plus a fusion mask; Google's FILM uses multi-scale feature extraction with shared weights to handle large motion and synthesises the frame from warped features rather than raw pixels, reducing tears.

Error is root-mean-square error over RGB in 0..1, shown as a percentage, against the exact frame rendered at time t from the procedural scene. The bar chart always uses t = ½, where interpolation is hardest.
