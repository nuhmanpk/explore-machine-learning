Challenges

No rules: set the weight to 0 and let it train until Spread is over 20. The dots sprawl far outside the circle, leaving holes.
Sweet spot: find a weight where junk is 30% or less and the pictures stay sharp (0.7 or more).
Too much tidying: set the weight to 3 and see sharpness drop to 0.65 or less.
Experimenter: train with 4 different weights.

What just happened?

  • This network is a variational autoencoder. Instead of one exact spot, it describes each picture as a fuzzy blob (a centre plus some random wobble), then rebuilds the picture from a random point inside that blob.
  • The tidy-up weight pulls all blobs towards the middle, so random spots land on something real. It is a trade: too little leaves holes and junk, too much makes every picture a grey, blurry average.
  • New pictures come from picking a random spot in the circle and decoding it. Nobody drew those pictures.
  • The junk and sharp numbers are measured by comparing each new picture with every real one, so they are not faked.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

A variational autoencoder (VAE) has an encoder that outputs a mean μ and log-variance log σ² for a 2-D Gaussian, and a decoder (2 → 32 → 64). Training minimises ‖x − x̂‖² + β · KL.

  • Reparameterisation trick: z = μ + σ · ε with ε ~ N(0, 1), so gradients flow through the sampling step into the encoder. This is implemented by hand here.
  • KL divergence to the prior N(0, I): −½ Σ (1 + log σ² − μ² − σ²). It is the regulariser. With β = 0 it is a plain autoencoder: the codes spread far outside the prior, so sampling from N(0, I) often lands in untrained gaps (junk).
  • Large β causes posterior collapse in the limit: the encoder ignores the input, and the decoder outputs the average picture.
  • This is a β-VAE view: the weight is on the KL term relative to the squared-error reconstruction term (a Gaussian likelihood with a fixed variance).
  • "Junk" = nearest training picture has per-pixel mean squared difference above 0.05. "Sharp" = mean of |pixel − 0.5| × 2: 1 for pure black and white, 0 for mid grey.

Further reading: Kingma and Welling, Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes (2014); Higgins et al., β-VAE (2017).
