What just happened?

  • The slow trend comes from averaging exactly one full season, so the ups and downs of the season cancel out.
  • Take the trend away and line up the pieces. Averaging every Monday, every Tuesday and so on gives the typical shape of the season.
  • What is left after removing trend and season is the noise. The better your length, the smaller that left-over looks.
  • Wrong lengths smear the pattern together so it averages to nothing. Double the real length also fits, because the pattern repeats twice.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is a classical additive decomposition, the idea behind STL: yt = trendt + seasont mod p + remaindert.

  • Trend: a centered moving average of length p (a 2×p average when p is even). Ends of the series, where the window does not fit, are held flat.
  • Season: for each position in the cycle, average the detrended values, then subtract their mean so the season sums to zero.
  • Season fit is the strength of seasonality max(0, 1 − Var(remainder) / Var(detrended)). It peaks at the true period and at its multiples. This scan is a crude cousin of the autocorrelation function and the periodogram.
  • Real STL uses LOESS smoothing so the seasonal shape can change slowly over time. Multiplicative seasonality (swings that grow with the level) is handled by taking logs first.
  • Seasonal structure is the main reason naive forecasts fail, which the next lesson tackles.

Further reading: Cleveland et al. (1990), STL: A Seasonal-Trend Decomposition Procedure Based on Loess.
