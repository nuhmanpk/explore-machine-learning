What just happened?

  • Each picture became just two numbers, so it could be a dot on a flat map. Nobody told the network which shapes exist, but similar shapes still landed near each other.
  • The second half of the network turns any spot on the map back into a picture, even a spot where no real picture was.
  • Sliding along the map gives a smooth change of shape. Plain cross-fading just makes a ghostly double image.
  • Far from the dots the drawings get odd, because the network never practised there. That is exactly the problem the next lesson fixes.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

The 2-unit bottleneck of this autoencoder (64 → 32 → 2 → 32 → 64) is a latent space: a learned low-dimensional coordinate system for the data. The encoder maps a picture to a point; the decoder maps any point back to a picture.

  • It is trained only to reconstruct. Clustering by shape is a side-effect: the encoder must give different pictures different codes, and similar pictures need similar codes to be rebuilt similarly.
  • "Tidy" is a leave-one-out k-nearest-neighbours check (k = 5) on the 360 training points. With 6 overlapping classes and only 2 dimensions, perfect is not expected.
  • Interpolation: decoding points on a straight line between two codes gives smooth in-betweens. Pixel cross-fading is linear in pixel space and just blends the two images.
  • A plain autoencoder places no rule on empty areas of the map, so decoded pictures there can be junk. Variational autoencoders add such a rule.

Further reading: Bengio, Courville and Vincent, Representation Learning: A Review and New Perspectives (2013).
