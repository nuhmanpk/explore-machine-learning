What just happened?

  • Every time you marked a message, the robot counted which words showed up in spam and which in fine messages.
  • Words like FREE and CLICK got a high spam score. Words like HOMEWORK and LUNCH got a high fine score.
  • To judge a message it adds up the scores of its words. Big total means probably spam.
  • The test messages were hidden, so the accuracy line is honest, just like a real exam.
  • Sneaky spellings and new topics fooled it, because it only knows the words it has seen. More labelled examples fix that.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is a multinomial Naive Bayes classifier on a bag of words: each message becomes a list of lowercase word counts and word order is ignored. "Naive" because it assumes words are independent given the class: P(spam | words) ∝ P(spam) · ∏ P(word | spam).

  • It is trained incrementally: each label just updates counts, so it is exact and instant. Un-marking a message subtracts its counts.
  • Laplace (add-one) smoothing: P(w | c) = (count(w, c) + 1) / (total words in c + V), so a word never seen in one class does not zero the whole product. V is the vocabulary size so far.
  • We work in log-odds: log P(spam)/P(fine) + Σ log P(w|spam)/P(w|fine). Sums are stable and each word's term is its colour-coded score. Probability = 1 / (1 + e−total). Words never seen are skipped.
  • Accuracy is measured on held-out messages. Misspellings and distribution shift (a new topic) break the word statistics; retraining on fresh labels repairs them.
  • Accuracy hides the kinds of error. Precision = of messages flagged spam, how many really were (false alarms hurt this). Recall = of real spam, how many were caught (misses hurt this). The next lesson digs into both.
