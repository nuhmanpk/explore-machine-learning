What's happening inside

Checkpoints — the Dreamer hive's beliefs saved along the way. Tap one to see what it thought back then.

What just happened?

  • No bee could see the nectar. Every hive had to try flowers to learn about them, and every try at a bad flower costs nectar.
  • The Random hive never learns, so it stays stuck around 50-60%.
  • The Greedy+dice hive mostly sticks with its favourite but keeps wasting a few visits on random flowers forever.
  • The Curious hive gives a bonus to flowers it knows little about. The Dreamer imagines how good each flower might be and goes with its best dream. Both quickly stop making mistakes: their "missed nectar" line goes flat.
  • When the meadow changed, hives that forget slowly noticed sooner. Remembering everything makes you stubborn!

How it works

Try

Visit a flower. Did it have nectar? Write it down.

Keep score

For each flower: how many visits, how much nectar. Few visits = still unsure.

Choose

Go to the best-looking flower, but give unsure flowers a chance too. That's the try or stick balance.

Repeat

The more you visit, the surer you get, and the less you need to explore.

For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is the multi-armed bandit problem: K arms (flowers) with unknown reward distributions, and the goal is to maximise total reward, i.e. minimise regret R(T) = Σt (μ* − μat), where μ* is the best arm's mean. The charts use expected (not sampled) regret, so lines are smooth. It is reinforcement learning with a single state: the pure exploration–exploitation trade-off.

  • Rewards: normal flowers are Bernoulli(p), one drop or none. The jackpot flower pays 4 drops with probability 0.15 (mean 0.6, high variance) so a lucky early jackpot can fool greedy estimates.
  • Random: uniform choice; linear regret.
  • ε-greedy: with probability ε pick uniformly, otherwise argmax of the sample mean. With fixed ε regret still grows linearly (≈ ε·average gap per step).
  • UCB1 (Auer et al. 2002): pick argmax of x̄i + c·bi·√(2 ln N / ni), untried arms first. Here c = 0.5 and bi is the arm's payout size (range scaling). Logarithmic regret.
  • Thompson sampling: Beta(1,1) priors, posterior Beta(1+successes, 1+failures). Each step draw θi ~ Beta and pick argmax θi·bi. Payouts in [0, b] use the Bernoulli trick (Agrawal & Goyal): a reward r counts as a success of weight r/b. The curves in "What's happening inside" are these posteriors.
  • Time of day makes the problem non-stationary: every 300 visits the arm means are swapped so a different flower is best. "Forget slowly" applies a discount γ = 0.99 to all counts each step (discounted UCB, Garivier & Moulines; discounted Thompson sampling), an effective memory of ≈ 1/(1−γ) = 100 visits. A sliding window of the last W visits works similarly.
  • New flower: a new arm with μ = 0.88. UCB tries it immediately (n = 0 gives an infinite bonus), Thompson's flat prior makes it likely to be sampled high, ε-greedy only finds it by its random ε steps.
  • Lai & Robbins showed no strategy can beat order log T regret on stationary bandits; UCB1 and Thompson sampling both achieve it. Bandits run real A/B tests, ad selection and clinical-trial designs.
