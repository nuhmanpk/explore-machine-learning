How it works

Words become numbers

Every word gets a list of numbers, plus a little number for where it sits in the sentence.

Ask and answer

The word it makes a question. Every other word holds up a label saying what it is.

Shine the spotlight

Questions that match labels get bright light. All the light adds up to 100%.

Nudge and repeat

Light on the right noun? Great. If not, the numbers get nudged so next time it's brighter.

What just happened?

  • At first Robo's spotlight was spread evenly over every word, like a flashlight with no aim.
  • Each practice round pushed more light onto the noun that it really meant.
  • A single clue word (hungry or scared, big or small) was enough to flip where Robo looked.
  • Chatbots do this for every word, many times, all at once. That is how they keep track of who is who.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is scaled dot-product attention, trained from scratch with analytic gradients and Adam. No pretrained model, nothing downloaded.

  • Query, key, value: each token vector is x = embedding + position. The asker's query is q = Wqc, every token has a key kj = Wkxj. Here the output we care about is the weights themselves; a full Transformer also mixes value vectors with them.
  • Weights: a = softmax(q·kj / √d) with d = 12. The softmax makes them sum to 1. Dividing by √d keeps scores from growing too big and saturating. Hidden words get a score of −∞, so their weight is exactly 0.
  • Loss: −log of the weight on the correct noun. Gradients flow into Wq, Wk, the word embeddings and the position vectors. They are checked against finite differences (relative error about 1e-8).
  • Positional information: attention itself ignores word order, so a learned vector per position is added. Here that is how "first noun" and "second noun" differ.
  • Simplification: in a real model a first layer lets "it" absorb the nearby clue word ("was hungry"). We feed c = eit + eclue directly, and only the "it" row is trained, so other rows in the heatmap are mostly noise.
  • Results: trained on 600 templated sentences, tested on 300 unseen ones, from about 40% (guessing) to 100% in roughly 100 rounds of 6 sentences. The weight on the correct noun goes from about 10% (1 / sentence length) to above 95%.
  • Real LLMs: use multi-head attention (many query/key/value sets looking for different things, like grammar, who-did-what, or nearby words), a causal mask so a word only sees earlier ones, and stack dozens of such layers, each followed by a small feed-forward network.
