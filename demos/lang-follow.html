How it works

Words become numbers

Each word gets its own list of numbers. At first they're random.

Look at the words

For every thing on the grid, Robo asks: which words are about me? That's attention.

Pick and walk

The thing that matches the words best gets the biggest chance. Robo walks toward it.

Treat or oops

Right thing = reward. Wrong thing = oops. The word numbers get nudged after every try.

What just happened?

  • At first, words were just random numbers to Robo, so it wandered to any old thing.
  • Every reward taught it a bit more about which words go with which colours and shapes.
  • Soon it paid attention to the important words (blue, star) and ignored filler (go, to, the).
  • Chatbots and robot helpers turn words into actions with this same attention trick, just much bigger.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

A tiny attention-based policy, trained from scratch by policy gradient. No pretrained model, nothing downloaded.

  • Embeddings: each of the 9 vocabulary tokens has a learned vector e ∈ ℝ⁸.
  • Queries: each object's features (colour + shape one-hots) are projected to a query q = W·f. Attention of object k on token t is softmax_t(q_k·e_t), shown as the glow under each word chip.
  • Grounding: object scores m_k = Σ_t q_k·e_t → p = softmax(m). Move probabilities mix "moves that approach object k" weighted by p_k (plus 4% exploration).
  • Learning: REINFORCE with a running baseline. Reward +1 for the right object, −1 for a wrong one, −0.5 for running out of steps. Gradients flow through p into both the embeddings and W.
  • From ~33% (chance with 3 objects) it typically reaches ~95% on 200 held-out tasks within ~1000 episodes (well under a second of compute).
  • Real systems (instruction-following agents, vision-language-action models) use the same ingredients: token embeddings, attention to ground words in what the agent sees, and RL or imitation to learn actions.
