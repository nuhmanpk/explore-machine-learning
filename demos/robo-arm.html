What just happened?

  • Forward kinematics goes from joint angles to hand position. It is just a bit of trigonometry.
  • Inverse kinematics goes backwards, from a target to angles. The law of cosines on the arm triangle gives the answer directly.
  • Most reachable targets have two solutions: elbow down and elbow up, mirror images of each other.
  • Targets farther than both links stretched, or closer than their difference, have no solution at all.
  • Real joints cannot spin freely, so limits can rule out one of the two solutions.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

Two links of length l1 = 100 and l2 = 70. Forward kinematics: x = l1 cos θ1 + l2 cos(θ1+θ2), y = l1 sin θ1 + l2 sin(θ1+θ2).

  • Analytic inverse kinematics: cos θ2 = (x² + y² − l1² − l2²) / (2 l1 l2), so θ2 = ± arccos(·) (the two elbow configurations). Then θ1 = atan2(y, x) − atan2(l2 sin θ2, l1 + l2 cos θ2).
  • The workspace is an annulus: reachable when |l1 − l2| ≤ √(x²+y²) ≤ l1 + l2. On the boundary the two solutions merge (a singularity).
  • Joint limits select among the analytic solutions. Arms with more joints than needed are redundant and have infinitely many solutions, so numerical methods (the Jacobian) are used.
  • This is classical control geometry, not learning. The games chapter shows how a learned controller can do a similar job without being given the formulas.
