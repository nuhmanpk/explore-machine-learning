    Challenges

    Ice cold: set the temperature to 0.1 or lower and press Repeat 5 times. All five stories should be exactly the same.
    Hot mess: temperature 2 or more, no top-k or top-p. Repeat 5 times: five different stories, and "makes sense" below 60%.
    Tame the heat: keep the temperature at 2 or more, but switch on top-p (60% or less) or top-k (5 or less). Repeat 5 times: at least 4 different stories that make sense 70% or more.

    What just happened?

    • The model looked at the last two words and listed every word it knows with a chance for each. The chances add up to 100%.
    • It picks the next word like a lucky dip: big bars are picked often, small bars sometimes.
    • Cold makes the biggest bar even bigger. At 0 it always picks the top word, so every try is the same, and it can get stuck in loops.
    • Hot squashes the bars flatter, so odd words sneak in. Fun at first, then nonsense.
    • Top-k and top-p throw the tiny bars out of the hat before the dice is rolled. That lets you be a bit hot and still make sense.
    • Big chat models do exactly this, one word piece at a time, with many thousands of possible pieces.

    How it works

    1. We read five little stories (about 4,000 words) and count which word comes after each pair of words, and after each single word.
    2. For the next word, we mix those counts: mostly "after these two words", some "after this one word", and a tiny bit of "how common is this word anyway".
    3. The temperature re-shapes the chances: each chance is raised to the power 1 ÷ temperature, then they are scaled to add to 100% again.
    4. Top-k keeps only the k biggest bars. Top-p keeps the biggest bars until they add up to p. The kept bars are scaled up to 100%.
    5. We roll a dice on what is left, add the word to the story, and repeat.
    For grown-ups: what's really going on

    The model is an interpolated trigram language model: P(w | u,v) = 0.6·P₃(w|u,v) + 0.37·P₂(w|v) + 0.03·P₁(w) with maximum-likelihood counts (weights renormalised when the trigram context is unseen). The unigram term gives every vocabulary word a small non-zero probability, which is what high temperature brings to the surface.

    • Temperature: treating z = log P as logits, sampling uses softmax(z / T), i.e. q ∝ P1/T. As T → 0 this tends to greedy decoding (argmax); T = 1 is the model's own distribution; T → ∞ tends to uniform. Here T ≤ 0.01 is treated as exact argmax.
    • Top-k sampling (Fan et al., 2018) truncates to the k most likely tokens and renormalises.
    • Nucleus (top-p) sampling (Holtzman et al., 2019) keeps the smallest set of most likely tokens whose cumulative probability is at least p, so the candidate set adapts: narrow when the model is confident, wide when it is not. Filters are applied after temperature, as in most LLM libraries.
    • "Different-ness" is distinct-2 (unique bigrams ÷ all bigrams across the five runs). "Makes sense" is a crude fluency proxy: the share of adjacent word pairs that occur in the training text. Real evaluations use perplexity under a stronger model or human judgement.

    Greedy decoding maximises per-step likelihood but is prone to repetition loops; this diversity–coherence trade-off is why production LLMs typically sample with T ≈ 0.7–1 plus top-p ≈ 0.9.
