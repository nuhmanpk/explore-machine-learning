How it works

Watch

The goalie remembers every kick: where it went, what came before, and whether you scored.

Look for clues

It has five little detectives. Each one has its own clue, like "what does this kicker do after scoring?"

Trust the good detectives

Detectives that guessed right get trusted more. Wrong ones get trusted less.

Dive

It dives where the trusted detectives point. Old memories slowly fade, so new habits get noticed.

What just happened?

  • The goalie started out guessing: about 1 save in 6, just luck.
  • Once it spotted your habits, its saves shot up.
  • When you changed your style, it got fooled for a while, then adapted to the new habit.
  • Kicking truly at random beats it, because there's no pattern to learn. That's not the goalie being silly: nothing could predict it!
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is online sequence prediction, the same idea as predicting the next word, but for penalty kicks.

  • Experts: five frequency models over the 6 zones with different contexts: none (marginal), the last kick (order-1 Markov), the last two kicks (order-2), the goalie's last dive, and the last kick plus whether it scored. Counts use add-0.3 smoothing.
  • Forgetting: each context's counts decay by 0.93 per observation, so the model tracks non-stationary behaviour (a changed habit).
  • Mixing: a multiplicative-weights / Hedge mixture: after each kick, expert e's weight is multiplied by (6·pe(zone))0.6, renormalised with a floor so dormant experts can return.
  • Decision: dive to the argmax of the mixed distribution. Chance is 1/6 ≈ 17%.
  • Curving ball: shots land one zone right of the aim with probability 0.6. The goalie only sees where the ball lands, so the best possible save rate is capped below 100%.
  • Against a truly uniform-random kicker no predictor can beat 1/6 in expectation, which is why mixed strategies are optimal in real penalty shoot-outs (game theory).
