How it works

Notes are words

To Robo, a song is a list of notes, like a sentence is a list of words.

Listen and count

For every note, Robo writes down what came right before it. The lines on the note map grow thicker.

Bet on the next note

To continue a tune, Robo looks at the last notes and bets on what usually comes next.

One note at a time

It picks a note, adds it to the tune, then bets again. Note by note, a new song appears!

What just happened?

  • Before any songs, Robo had nothing to go on, so it could only play random notes.
  • Each song it heard added more "this note comes after that note" counts. You saw the note map fill up.
  • The checkpoints show its tunes getting more song-like as it learned more songs.
  • Remembering more notes makes it sound more like the real songs. Remember too many and it just copies them!
  • Big music AIs play this same guess-the-next-piece game, with far more music and much bigger brains.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is a Markov chain / n-gram language model over musical tokens, trained by counting.

  • Tokens: each note is a token pitch:duration (e.g. E4:1, G4:2). The songs are traditional, public-domain melodies, simplified.
  • Model: for context length k (the “notes Robo remembers” slider), it stores counts c(context → next). Prediction is P(next | last k) = c / Σc, with backoff to shorter contexts when a context was never seen.
  • Sampling: probabilities are reshaped with a temperature T: pi ∝ pi1/T. Low T → greedy and repetitive; high T → random.
  • Memorisation vs generalisation: with k = 3 most contexts appear in only one song, so the model reproduces that song verbatim. With k = 1 it mixes songs freely but loses structure.

Real music models use the same next-token idea at scale. MusicGen (Meta) and MusicLM (Google) compress raw audio into discrete tokens with a neural audio codec (EnCodec / SoundStream), then a transformer predicts the next audio token, conditioned on a text description. Instead of counting, a transformer learns smooth, long-range patterns (rhythm, harmony, repetition), so it generalises far beyond the songs it heard.
