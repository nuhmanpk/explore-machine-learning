What's happening inside

Challenges

Find the sweet spot: the level with the smallest red test error.
Make a memoriser: level 9 or more, great on study dots, bad on test dots. Try fewer dots.
Tame it: give it 30+ examples and keep the wiggle at 9 or more. Overfitting shrinks!

What just happened?

  • Too stiff a curve misses the pattern: it is wrong on study dots and test dots. That is underfitting.
  • Too wiggly a curve hits every study dot but wobbles wildly between them. That is memorising.
  • The honest score is on dots the model never studied, the hollow test dots.
  • The best wiggle level is where the test error is lowest, the bottom of the U.
  • More examples or less noise make the wiggly curve behave better.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is polynomial regression of degree d (wiggle level) fit by least squares: solve the normal equations (XᵀX + λI) w = Xᵀy with a tiny ridge term λ = 10⁻⁶ for stability. Inputs are scaled to [-1, 1] and a Chebyshev basis is used so the matrix stays well-conditioned.

  • Training error only falls as d grows (nested models), reaching zero once d + 1 ≥ n. Test (generalisation) error is U-shaped.
  • The bias-variance trade-off: low degree means high bias (underfitting); high degree means high variance (overfitting), the curve changes a lot with each new sample of noise.
  • Errors shown are RMSE. Picking the level from the test dots is a form of validation; a real project also keeps a final untouched test set, or uses cross-validation.
  • Remedies: more data, regularisation (larger λ), simpler models, early stopping.
