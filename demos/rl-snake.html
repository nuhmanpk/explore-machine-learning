How it works

Feel

The snake only feels a few things: is there danger ahead, left or right, and which way the apple is.

Check the cheat-sheet

For each mix of feelings, its table has scores for "straight", "left" and "right". It picks the biggest.

Treats and ouches

An apple is a big treat. Bumping a wall or its tail is a big ouch. Wandering costs a tiny bit.

Fix the scores

After every move it nudges that score toward what really happened next. Hundreds of games later, it hunts apples.

What just happened?

  • The first snakes crashed into walls almost right away. Their tables were empty.
  • Each crash lowered the score for "that move, when I felt that". Each apple raised the score for the moves that led there.
  • After a few hundred games, the robot snake eats 15–20 apples a game, all learned from treats and ouches.
  • It still sometimes traps itself in its own tail. With only 7 feelings it can't plan far ahead. Smarter feelings (or a neural network) would help.
For grown-ups: what's really going on

Another tabular Q-learning agent, this time with a hand-designed, relative state.

  • State (11 bits → at most 2,048 cells): danger straight / right / left (relative to heading), heading (4 values), and food up / down / left / right.
  • Actions: straight, turn right, turn left. Relative actions mean the snake can never reverse into itself.
  • Reward: +10 per apple, −10 for dying, −0.01 per step. A game also ends if the snake wanders too long without eating. α = 0.3, γ = 0.9.
  • Exploration: with auto curiosity, ε decays from 0.1 to 0.005 over the first 300 games.
  • Offline in Node over 3 runs: 50-game average ≈ 3 apples after 100 games, ≈ 8 after 200, ≈ 18 after 400. The greedy policy then averages 17–19 apples. Long snakes still die by coiling into themselves, because the state can't see the body beyond one cell.
  • Deep RL versions (DQN) feed the whole grid into a neural network and can learn longer-range planning.
