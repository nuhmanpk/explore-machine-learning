How it works

You design the body

Joints are like elbows, bones are stiff sticks, muscles are stretchy bands that can squeeze.

A tiny brain squeezes

Each creature has a little brain. It feels its tilt, its height, which feet touch the ground and a beat, then tells every muscle how hard to squeeze.

40 try at once

At first the brains are random, so the creatures flop and twitch.

The best become parents

The ones that go furthest (or jump highest, or climb best) get copied, with small random changes.

Walking appears

Nobody tells them how to walk. Generation after generation, a way of moving that fits YOUR creature appears by itself.

What just happened?

  • You invented a body, and evolution invented a way for that exact body to move.
  • Random brains made the creatures flop. Keeping the best and copying them with small changes made them better, generation after generation.
  • A challenge like ice or a floppy muscle knocked the score down, then the brains adapted.
  • Weird bodies get weird (and funny) walks. Evolution uses whatever works!
For grown-ups: what's really going on

This is neuroevolution of a locomotion controller for a body you design.

  • Physics: Verlet integration. Joints are point masses; bones are rigid distance constraints; muscles are soft distance constraints whose rest length is L₀ · (1 − 0.32·s), where s ∈ [−1, 1] is the squeeze. Six relaxation passes per step, ground friction applied to joints in contact, gravity, optional wind.
  • Brain: one fully connected tanh network per creature, [15 → 10 → muscles]. Inputs: body tilt (sin, cos), height above ground, horizontal and vertical speed, ground contact of up to 8 joints, and a 1.4 Hz clock (sin, cos) that makes rhythms easy to evolve. It runs every other physics step.
  • Evolution: 40 creatures, a 10 s race per generation, fitness = distance (plus height bonuses for the jump and stairs goals). The top ~8% survive unchanged (elitism), the rest are mutated copies or crossovers of the top quarter, with a spread of mutation sizes. The simulation is deterministic, so with elitism the best score can only go up until a challenge changes the world.
  • Lose a muscle disables the muscle the current champion uses most, so the remaining muscles must learn a new gait.

Inspired by Karl Sims' Evolved Virtual Creatures (1994) and Keiwan Donyagard's game Evolution.
